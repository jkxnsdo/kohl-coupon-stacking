## The stacking order is the part everyone gets wrong

Kohl's prints this in the fine print of every Kohl's Cash coupon, and it explains most of the confusion around coupon stacking: **Kohl's Cash and other dollar-off coupons are applied before percent-off total-purchase coupons.**

Translation: a $10-off-$50 coupon comes off first, then your 20% off applies to the remaining balance. That's a good outcome — the percent-off discount is calculated on the pre-discount subtotal rather than shrinking because of the dollar-off code. But it also means the marginal value of that extra code isn't as large as it looks, since you pay tax on the discounted amount and Kohl's Cash earnings are calculated after all coupons come off.

Two more mechanical details that trip people up:

- **Six Kohl's Cash coupons maximum per transaction.** If you've been hoarding $5 Rewards certificates all month, they don't all go through.
- **Kohl's Cash earning is based on the amount after every coupon**, not on your original subtotal. Stack three codes on a $60 order and you may drop under the $50 threshold entirely — no Kohl's Cash at all.

## Kohl's Cash: the coupon that isn't a coupon

Kohl's Cash is the piece of the system that changes how much a purchase actually costs, and it's the one people most often miss because it isn't a code you paste in — it's a reward you earn by shopping in a specific window.

The current earn event runs **September 28 through October 4, 2026** (Central Time). Spend $50 in a single transaction and you get $10 in Kohl's Cash; spend $100, get $20; and so on. It applies to sale, regular and clearance merchandise. You then have to come back and spend it between **October 5 and October 18, 2026**, or it evaporates.

A couple of practical notes:

- Online orders earn Kohl's Cash by email, usually within 24 hours. Save that email — Kohl's doesn't resend it, and the coupon number and PIN live inside it. In-store purchases print at the register.
- Kohl's Cash works on almost everything coupons won't touch. That's the real play. It can't be redeemed on Sephora merchandise, gift cards, price adjustments, Kohl's Card payments, or fees like tax and shipping — but it *can* be spent on the excluded apparel brands a percent-off code can't reach.
- Timing matters more than almost anything else. Waiting a week to hit an earn window can be worth $20 or $30 on a bigger purchase. According to Groupon's Kohl's guide, spending $48 still rounds up to the $50 threshold, so you don't need to nail the exact number.

## Kohl's Rewards runs year-round, which is why it's easy to ignore

Kohl's Cash earn events are the loud part. Kohl's Rewards is the quiet part: the free loyalty program pays **5% back on every purchase**, and holders of the Kohl's Card earn 7.5%. Rewards accrue year-round, then convert into Kohl's Cash in $5 increments and land on the first of the following month. Those Rewards-issued certificates are only good for 30 days, so they're easy to lose if you don't check your Kohl's Wallet.

Enrollment is free and it stacks with earn events. On a $150 purchase during an earn window, the math gets interesting fast: roughly $30 in event Kohl's Cash plus about $7.50 in Rewards on top. The catch is that the second half arrives next month and expires in 30 days, so it only pays off if you shop at Kohl's more than once a season.

## Things that don't combine, so stop trying

- **Kohl's coupons + Sephora at Kohl's.** Nothing applies, in either direction.
- **Kohl's Cash + gift card payment.** A gift-card-funded portion earns no cashback, and if a gift card covers the entire order, the cashback is zero.
- **Kohl's Cash redemption + Sephora or gift card purchases.** Both excluded.
- **Coupons + a percentage of the sale that never existed.** Kohl's Cash is calculated after coupons, so stacking aggressively past the $50 line can cost you the Kohl's Cash you were shopping for in the first place.
